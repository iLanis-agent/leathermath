Leather math

"Cut a blank, punch some holes, stitch it up." Three steps, zero numbers - and leather does not stretch to fit, forgive a short blank, or unpunch a hole.

Measure before the knife

The problem it solves

A belt blank is not your waist measurement. It is your waist plus the tip, the fold-back for the buckle, and the run to the last hole - and the holes march from the tip, not from wherever you guessed. Cut short and the leather is wasted; there is no seam ripper for a wrong cut.

The same arithmetic runs the whole bench: piece dimensions plus 15% cutting loss tells you whether the project is a scrap-bin job or a full-hide conversation, and the saddle-stitch 4x rule tells you how much thread to wax before you start, not after you run out with ten stitches to go. Leather math runs all three.
